How to source ginseng extract without guessing the ginsenoside grade

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 5 November 2026 · 6 min read

What we establish before quoting a ginseng extract order. Most ginseng enquiries arrive with a competitor price and no sheet, so the first thing we establish is the assay basis: ours is total ginsenosides at 5% / 10% / 20% / 80%, read by UV with HPLC available on request, and a price quoted against a material whose percentage is measured a different way cannot be compared with it. We then ask about root age, because our standard material comes from cultivated 4–6 year roots and that line is where assay drift starts. We ask whether the buyer needs Rg1 + Rb1 ratio declarations, and which grade colour and solubility the format needs — white to light-yellow for capsules, water-soluble grades for beverages. Packing comes next: a 25 kg fibre drum as standard, foil bags for pilots, OEM under the buyer’s own brand if needed.

Ginseng extract is one of the most purchased botanicals in the industry, and one of the easiest to mis-buy, because the market uses the same name for materials that differ in assay basis, root age and extraction system. The grade we supply is a root extract of Panax ginseng C.A.Meyer standardised to total ginsenosides, available from 5% up to 80%, a white to light-yellow fine powder from cultivated 4–6 year roots. This guide covers how to source it: what the material is, the specification line by line, how the grades differ, and what a first order looks like. If you are comparing several suppliers at once, the mechanics below are the same ones we apply to every ginseng extract quotation that leaves this desk.

What ginseng extract is

The source line on the sheet reads Panax ginseng C.A.Meyer, root, cultivated 4–6 years, and the process line reads water / ethanol-water extraction. The assay line reads total ginsenosides at 5% / 10% / 20% / 80%, read by UV, with HPLC available on request. Two details matter before prices are compared. First, the root age: 4–6 year cultivated roots are the stated standard, and that is the line that separates consistent material from lots whose ginsenoside content drifts between harvests. Second, the assay basis: the percentage is measured on the batch that ships and reported on the lot COA, rather than quoted as a generic claim.

The physical lines are the ones buyers skip and then ask about later. Loss on drying is limited to 5.0% or less, and together with the assay that is what the lot COA reports against. A typical COA for a recent standard lot read ginsenosides 24.6% by UV, loss on drying 4.4%, lead below 1 ppm, total plate count below 1,000 cfu/g, with E. coli and Salmonella negative — indicative values, because the COA that matters is the one issued with your batch. Rg1 + Rb1 ratio declarations are available on request.

How the grades differ: 5% to 80%, white and water-soluble

The four assay grades are four different formulation tools. The low assays — 5% and 10% — are the grades buyers choose for ginseng teas, tonics and cost-driven blends, where the botanical character matters and the concentrate works at higher inclusion rates. The 20% grade is the all-rounder that covers most capsule and tablet work. The 80% grade is the high-potency concentrate for compact capsule fills where every milligram of fill weight counts. Across the range, appearance runs white to light-yellow, and water-soluble grades are produced for beverage work.

What ginseng extract is bought for

Five applications cover almost every enquiry we see:

  • Energy and focus stacks — the home application; the 20% and 80% grades carry the label math in compact fills
  • Immune-support blends — combined with other botanicals, where the Rg1 + Rb1 declaration supports the formulator’s documentation
  • Sports nutrition formulas — blended into pre-workout and recovery bases
  • Energy drinks — water-soluble grades dispersed in the mix step
  • Ginseng teas and tonics — the 5% and 10% grades, where the botanical character leads

What changes between these formats is not the material but the grade and the trial. One compliance point we put in writing on every ginseng quotation: we supply the extract as a raw ingredient for manufacturing and make no efficacy claims for it — how the finished product is labelled and positioned is the buyer’s responsibility. Adaptogen-minded formulators often shortlist it alongside our Rhodiola Rosea Extract and Epimedium Extract, which ship under the same QC standard.

Frequently asked questions

What is ginseng extract standardised to?
The standard grades are total ginsenosides at 5% / 10% / 20% / 80%, read by UV with HPLC available on request, from the root of Panax ginseng C.A.Meyer — cultivated 4–6 year roots, extracted with water or ethanol-water. The powder is white to light-yellow, fine, at 80 mesh, with loss on drying limited to 5.0% or less. Rg1 + Rb1 ratio declarations are available on request.

What should I check when comparing ginseng extract specifications?
Check the assay basis first: the total ginsenosides percentage and the method behind it (UV, with HPLC on request), because sheets written on different bases cannot be compared line by line. Then the root age line (cultivated 4–6 year roots), whether Rg1 + Rb1 ratio declarations are offered, the grade colour and water solubility the format needs, the mesh line (80 mesh standard), and the physical limits. Ask each supplier for the lot COA that would ship with your order rather than a generic sample.

What is the MOQ, sample policy and lead time for ginseng extract?
MOQ is 1 kg. Samples are always drawn from production lots; the free quantity depends on the item's value, and anything beyond is billed at cost plus courier and quoted up front. In-stock items are ready in about 2 days, bulk production takes 3 to 5 working days after deposit, and transit is about 3 days Xi'an to Shenzhen plus about 6 days by air to major U.S. and EU airports, or 18 to 25 days by sea.
