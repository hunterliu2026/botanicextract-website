Where chrysanthemum powder earns its place in a formula

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 20 October 2026 · 6 min read

The formulation brief we ask for before recommending a grade. Most chrysanthemum enquiries arrive with a competitor's label and the question: can you supply this. Before answering we ask three things: which other botanicals share the formula, whether the label quantifies a marker, and the dose form. A capsule blend built around cassia and goji and a bottled wellness tea are different specifications, and choosing the wrong basis, ratio when the label needs flavonoids by UV or the reverse, is the most expensive mistake made with this ingredient.

Chrysanthemum powder is a niche with a clear shape. The product page lists two application areas, food and beverage and dietary supplements, and almost every enquiry we see lands in one of them. What changes between the two is not the material but the specification basis and the way a pilot should be run. This article covers both, and the questions to settle before a formula is locked. For the buying mechanics behind these choices — what a quotation actually covers and how a first order is structured — see our chrysanthemum powder sourcing guide.

Food and beverage: teas, drinks and syrups

This is the material’s home. On the product page it is listed for herbal tea blends, hot and ready-to-drink, floral wellness beverages, and honey-chrysanthemum syrups. The classic preparation in East Asia is a diluted flower tea, and the powder is the concentrated form of exactly that. For these formats the 10:1 grade is usually the right starting point: the product is a traditional blend, the label does not quantify a marker, and the ratio basis keeps the cost structure honest.

Two practical notes from beverage work:

  • Dissolution. At 80 mesh the powder disperses readily in a dry blend or a bottling line’s mixing step. For a clear bottled tea, plan a filtration or clarification step, because a flower extract will otherwise leave a fine sediment that reads as a defect on shelf.
  • Taste at serving strength. The floral character is part of the product, not a defect, but a sample spooned dry tells you nothing about how it carries in the finished dilution. Trial at serving strength, not at powder strength.

Dietary supplements: eye-health and calming blends

The second area is capsules, tablets and stick packs. Two clusters dominate:

  • Eye-health formulas. Chrysanthemum has been formulated alongside cassia seed extract, goji, lutein and zeaxanthin in screen-fatigue and vision-support blends for as long as the category has existed. This is the cluster where most of our enquiries sit, and it is the natural cross-sell when a buyer already runs cassia.
  • Calming and relaxation blends. Paired with ingredients such as tea L-theanine or GABA in evening and wind-down products, where a gentle floral character suits the positioning better than a bitter botanical.

Two situations push a buyer to the marker basis instead of the 10:1 grade:

  • The label states a figure. If the panel declares total flavonoids, the 10:1 ratio cannot support it. Specify 2% or 5% by UV instead.
  • The quality file needs enforceability. A ratio is not a test result. If your documentation must show a potency number per lot, the UV grades come with one on the COA, and the typical COA shows a standard lot running at 2.4%.

Running a pilot before the formula is locked

Order the pilot as a 1 kg or 5 kg foil bag drawn from a production lot, so the material you trial is the material that would ship. MOQ is 1 kg, in-stock items are ready in about 2 days, and bulk production of standard grades takes 3 to 5 working days after deposit, so a pilot does not need to sit on the critical path for long. Run two checks in the trial: the assay result against your intended basis, and the flavour or aroma in the finished format, at serving strength for a beverage and at finished dose for a capsule.

A tea buyer and a capsule buyer can order the same flower on different bases and both be right. The basis follows the label, not the ingredient.

One last mechanical point that applies to both areas. Because the material is sold as a raw ingredient for manufacturing, the pack you receive is a 25 kg fibre drum with a double polyethylene liner for bulk, or a 1 kg or 5 kg foil bag for samples and pilot runs, and the shelf life is 24 months unopened. Plan the trial inside that window: a pilot that sits on a shelf for two quarters has consumed a third of its life before the formula is even locked.

Frequently asked questions

What formats is chrysanthemum powder used in?
The product page lists two areas: food and beverage, including herbal tea blends hot and ready-to-drink, floral wellness beverages and honey-chrysanthemum syrups, and dietary supplements, including eye-health formulas with cassia and goji and calming or relaxation blends.

Should a tea blend use the 10:1 grade or the flavonoids grades?
Use the 10:1 grade when the product is a classic blend and the label does not quantify a marker. Use total flavonoids at 2% or 5% by UV when the panel states a figure or your quality file needs a per-lot potency number. We supply both and confirm feasibility before quoting.
