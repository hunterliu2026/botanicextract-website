Blueberry Powder Buyer's Guide: Grades, Carriers and COA Checks

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 17 October 2026 · 6 min read

From our own quoting desk When a buyer opens with "blueberry powder price?" we genuinely cannot quote yet. A carrier-free juice-solids powder and a maltodextrin-diluted version cost very different amounts to produce, and 100 mesh runs on a different line from 80 mesh. Our first reply is always two questions: what does your label need to claim, and what is the end format? Once those two answers are in, a firm specification and price follow within one business day.

Ask five suppliers for "blueberry powder" and you may receive five different materials. One is a pure spray-dried juice concentrate with the color of the fresh fruit. One is mostly maltodextrin with enough blueberry to tint it. One is a flavored blend that never touched a blueberry at all. All three are sold legally, all three appear in ingredient directories, and only one of them will survive your marketing team's ingredient statement.

Three products share the name

The fastest way to separate them is a single question: is it carrier-free, and what does the COA say? A supplier who cannot answer that in one email is selling the third category.

What "pure concentration powder" tells you

Our standard grade is specified as a pure concentration powder with no synthetic colors, spray-dried at low temperature from fresh juice and pulp. Notice what that specification does not include: a fixed assay percentage. Extracts like silymarin are sold at a defined strength; a whole-fruit powder is not, because its value is carried by color and flavor rather than a single marker compound.

For buyers, that shifts the conversation. Instead of asking "what percentage?", ask for the color value and anthocyanin profile on the batch COA. Those two numbers tell you what the powder will actually do in your product, and they are the figures worth comparing across suppliers.

Rule of thumb: for whole-fruit powders, the COA's color value matters more than any percentage on a price list.

Carrier choice drives your label

We supply the standard grade carrier-free, and produce a maltodextrin-carrier version on request. This is not a cost footnote, it is a labeling decision:

  • Carrier-free keeps a "100% blueberry fruit" statement clean on your ingredient panel and delivers the deepest color per gram.
  • Maltodextrin carrier improves powder flow, lowers cost per kilo, and works well where color intensity is not the selling point.

Decide the label claim first, then the carrier. Buying the cheaper version and discovering your packaging already promises "no added carriers" is an expensive way to learn this.

Mesh size matters more than buyers expect

The standard particle size is 80 mesh, with 100 mesh available on request. The difference shows up in the finished product, not on the spec sheet:

  • 80 mesh suits gummies, confectionery, bakery and cereal coatings, where a fine but perceptible texture is acceptable and solubility is not critical.
  • 100 mesh suits smoothie blends, instant beverages and direct-compressed tablets, where fast dispersion and a smooth mouthfeel matter.

Finer powder also absorbs moisture faster, which is why the moisture limit matters: our specification holds loss on drying at 5.0% or below, and the unopened 25 kg drum with a double polyethylene liner is what keeps the 24-month shelf life intact.

Reading the COA: the numbers that matter

A serious certificate of analysis for this grade will show at minimum:

  • Loss on drying ≤ 5.0% — excess moisture causes caking and shortens shelf life in humid climates.
  • Total ash ≤ 5.0% — a proxy for mineral load and process cleanliness.
  • Full microbiological panel — ask for it explicitly; it is not always printed on a summary COA.
  • Color value for the specific lot — this is what you compare batch to batch, and supplier to supplier.

If a COA arrives with only a visual description of the color, you are looking at a trading company's summary, not a production record.

Packing, MOQ and lead time

Plan the first order around these real numbers:

  • Packing: 25 kg fibre drum with double PE liner (standard); 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags for samples and pilot runs.
  • MOQ: OEM orders from 1 kg.
  • Lead time: in-stock items ready in about 2 days; bulk production 3–5 working days after deposit for standard grades.
  • Transit: Xi'an to Shenzhen consolidation about 3 days, then air freight about 6 days to major U.S. and EU airports; sea freight 18–25 days.

For a first order, the honest total from deposit to U.S. warehouse is roughly two weeks by air. Buyers who plan a launch on a three-day shipping assumption are usually disappointed.

Five documents to request before your first order

  1. The full specification sheet (PDF) for the exact grade you are pricing.
  2. A sample Certificate of Analysis — and the current production lot's actual COA, not a generic example.
  3. A sample drawn from a production lot, so you test the real material rather than a hand-made specimen.
  4. The complete documentation list that ships with the quotation.
  5. Packing and storage conditions in writing, including shelf life from the production date.

A supplier who provides all five without being chased is a supplier whose production records are in order. That is the real test of a first order.

Frequently asked questions

Is your blueberry powder 100% fruit?
Our standard grade is a pure concentration powder, spray-dried from blueberry juice and pulp with no synthetic colors and no carrier, so a "100% fruit" statement is supportable. We also produce a maltodextrin-carrier version on request for applications where cost matters more than the label claim.

Which mesh size should I choose, 80 or 100?
80 mesh is the standard and suits gummies, confectionery and bakery or cereal coatings. 100 mesh is available on request and is the better choice for smoothie blends, instant beverages and tablets where fast dispersion and a smooth mouthfeel matter.

Can I get a sample before ordering?
Yes. Samples are always drawn from production lots, so you test the real material rather than a specimen made for sampling. Samples ship in 1 kg or 5 kg foil bags; how much we can supply free of charge depends on the item's value, and anything beyond that is billed at cost plus courier and quoted up front.

What is the anthocyanin content?
As a whole-fruit powder, the standard grade is not sold against a fixed marker-compound assay. Color value and the anthocyanin profile are reported on the batch COA, which is what you should compare across suppliers. If your label claim requires a specific figure, tell us the target and we will confirm what the current lots actually deliver.
