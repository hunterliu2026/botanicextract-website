How to read a gypenoside specification, line by line

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 9 November 2026 · 6 min read

What we settle before a gypenoside specification sheet goes out. Most gypenoside enquiries arrive with a competitor sheet quoting a single percentage, so the first thing we establish is the assay level and method, because sheets written at 20% and 98% — or on different assay methods — cannot be compared line by line. We then ask which market the powder is going into, since mass-market functional foods and premium supplements pull the grade and the carrier line in different directions, and we confirm the mesh the process needs, 80 mesh as standard or 100 mesh on request, before the sheet goes out. We also ask our manufacturing partner for the most recent lot COA to send alongside the sheet: the sheet states limits, the COA states results, and on a marginal lot those are two different documents. If the buyer needs an alternative mesh size or carrier system, we confirm feasibility and lead time with the partner within one business day rather than quoting blind.

A gypenoside specification is one of the shorter sheets on our list, and it is also the one where two buyers can hold two different documents under the same product name, because the category ships at two assay levels. Most enquiries arrive quoting a single percentage — 20% or 98% — without the assay method or the carrier line, and those two details decide whether the sheet can be compared with ours. This article reads the gypenoside specification in the order that matters: what the source and process lines tell you, what the 20% and 98% UV assays commit to, what the physical lines decide, and what the batch COA adds. The buying mechanics are covered in our gypenoside sourcing guide.

What the source and process lines tell you

The source line reads jiaogulan (Gynostemma pentaphyllum), the climbing herb known as “southern ginseng”, and the part used is the herb itself. The process line reads water / ethanol-water extraction, and that combination is what pulls the gypenosides — the ginseng-like saponins the material is standardised on — into the extract. Two details matter here. First, the botanical line: jiaogulan is the stated raw material, and it is what the “gypenoside” on the label ultimately commits to. Second, the extraction line: water-only and ethanol-water lots behave differently in solution, so if your finished format is sensitive to taste or colour, tell us which extraction route your process assumes before quotations are compared. The result either way is a yellow-brown to tan fine powder with characteristic odour and taste.

The assay line: what 20% and 98% UV commits to

The assay line reads gypenosides 20% or 98%, measured by UV spectrophotometry. That is the single most useful filter in the category, and the two grades exist for different markets rather than as a quality ladder. The 20% grade is the standard production grade for mass-market functional foods, where a higher assay would push the inclusion cost out of line. The 98% grade is what premium supplements and research buyers specify, because their label claims are written against the higher figure. Both figures are UV results, so when a competitor sheet quotes a gypenoside percentage measured by a different method, the numbers cannot be compared line by line — ask which method their sheet is written against first.

The assay line is a commitment, not a result. The sheet states what the grade commits to; the batch COA states what the lot tested.

The physical lines: carrier, mesh, loss on drying and ash

Four physical lines decide whether the gypenoside powder works in your process:

  • Carrier. Our standard grade is a pure extract with no carrier; maltodextrin can be added on request where a free-flowing, diluted powder suits the blending step better. Ask for the carrier line to be stated explicitly on any sheet you compare.
  • Particle size. 80 mesh is the standard grade, with 100 mesh on request for processes that need a finer dispersion.
  • Loss on drying. Limited to 5.0% or less. This is the free-moisture line, and it matters most for shelf life in unopened original packing.
  • Total ash. Also limited to 5.0% or less. On a saponin extract the ash limit keeps the assay honest, because inorganic residue would otherwise inflate the weight.

When two gypenoside specifications land side by side, the assay method and the carrier line are where they diverge first, and the batch COA is the document that settles which sheet describes the material you would actually receive.

How we read a gypenoside specification against a quotation

When a buyer sends us a competitor sheet, we read four things in this order: the assay level and method, the carrier line, the physical limits, and the documentation offered. Where the sheet quotes a percentage without a method, we ask for the lot COA behind it. Where the carrier differs from our pure-extract standard, we confirm feasibility and lead time with our manufacturing partner within one business day rather than quoting blind. And where the buyer needs OEM packing under their own brand, we handle that as drum and label work, not as a change to the powder. The markets each grade is bought for — mass-market functional foods at 20%, premium supplements and research at 98% — are covered in our gypenoside applications breakdown.

Frequently asked questions

What is gypenoside standardised to?
The standard grades are gypenosides 20% and 98%, both measured by UV spectrophotometry, from jiaogulan (Gynostemma pentaphyllum) herb extracted with water or ethanol-water. The powder is yellow-brown to tan and fine, with characteristic odour and taste. Loss on drying and total ash are each limited to 5.0% or less, and the powder is 80 mesh as standard with 100 mesh on request.

Should I choose the 20% or the 98% gypenoside grade?
The 20% grade is what most buyers in mass-market functional foods use, because it keeps the inclusion cost workable in everyday formats. The 98% grade is the one specified for premium supplements and research work, where the label claim needs the higher assay. Both grades are UV-assayed and shipped with a batch COA, and we confirm which grade buyers in your category use, usually within one email.

What packing and shelf life does gypenoside come in?
Standard packing is a 25 kg fibre drum with a double polyethylene liner, with 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags for samples and pilot runs. Shelf life is 24 months in unopened original packing, stored cool, dry and away from direct sunlight. MOQ is 1 kg, and every lot ships with a batch COA.
