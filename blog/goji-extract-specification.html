How to read a goji extract specification, line by line

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 7 November 2026 · 6 min read

What we settle before a goji extract specification sheet goes out. Most goji enquiries arrive with a competitor sheet quoting a single polysaccharide number, so the first thing we establish is the grade: goji polysaccharides 30%, 40% or 50%, all measured by UV-VIS, because a 30% sheet and a 50% sheet are different materials at different prices and cannot be compared line by line. We then ask which application the powder is going into, since superfood powders and beverage bases, supplement blends and colour-forward skincare pull the grade and the documentation in different directions, and whether the process needs the pure grade with no added carriers or a standard spray-dried powder. We also ask our manufacturing partner for the most recent lot COA to send alongside the sheet: the sheet states limits, the COA states results, and on a marginal lot those are two different documents. If the formula needs zeaxanthin stated, we confirm that the zeaxanthin content line can be added to the sheet before quotations are compared.

A goji extract specification is a short sheet, and it is read in the wrong order more often than in almost any other category, because the market sells everything from juice powders to concentrated polysaccharide grades under the same name. This article reads the goji extract specification in the order that matters: what the source and process lines tell you, what the polysaccharide assay actually commits to, what the physical lines decide, and what the batch COA adds on top. The commercial side — how quotations are compared and what a first order looks like — is covered in our goji extract sourcing guide.

What the source and process lines tell you

The source line reads Lycium barbarum L., fruit. The process line is where the value is concentrated: the raw material is sun-dried fruit, extracted with water (food grade) as the solvent, then spray-dried into a powder that is freely soluble in water. Two details matter more than they look. First, the fruit is concentrated rather than simply dried and milled, which is what separates a goji extract from the ground fruit powders sold at lower assay levels. Second, the pure grade carries no added carriers, so the polysaccharide percentage you read on the assay line is not diluted by maltodextrin declared somewhere else on the sheet.

The assay line: goji polysaccharides 30% / 40% / 50% (UV-VIS)

The assay line offers three standard grades: goji polysaccharides at 30%, 40% or 50%, each measured by UV-VIS. The number and the method travel together on our sheet, and that pairing is the first thing to check on any competitor sheet, because a percentage quoted without its method cannot be compared with one that can. The grade is the single most useful filter in the category: beverage and superfood formats most often run the 30% or 40% grades, while formulations positioned around a higher polysaccharide declaration take the 50% grade. Alternative assays can be produced to your specification — we confirm feasibility and lead time with our manufacturing partner before quoting rather than after.

The assay line is a commitment, not a result. The sheet states what the grade commits to; the batch COA states what the lot tested.

The physical lines: appearance, mesh and loss on drying

Three physical lines decide whether the powder works in your process:

  • Appearance. Orange-red to red-brown fine powder. This is the line buyers check against the sample rather than against the sheet, because the colour is what carries through into the finished product.
  • Particle size. 80 mesh as standard. At 80 mesh the powder blends into dry matrices and disperses into beverage bases without a separate milling stage.
  • Loss on drying. Limited to 5.0% or less. This is the free-moisture line, and it is the one that matters most for shelf life in unopened original packing.

Zeaxanthin content is available on request, which matters for eye-health formulations that want the carotenoid stated on the label documentation rather than implied by the botanical name.

When two goji extract specifications land side by side, the COA is the document that settles which sheet describes the material you would actually receive.

How we read a goji specification against a quotation

When a buyer sends us a competitor sheet, we read four things in this order: the assay grade and its method, the carrier status, the physical lines, and the documentation offered. Where the competitor sheet quotes a single percentage without a UV-VIS statement, we ask for the lot COA behind it. Where the grade differs from the one the buyer’s formula was costed on, we re-quote rather than pretend the sheets match. And where the buyer needs OEM packing under their own brand, we treat that as drum and label work, not as a change to the powder. The formats the powder is bought for — superfood blends, supplement formulas and colour-forward skincare — are covered in our goji extract applications breakdown.

Frequently asked questions

What is goji extract standardised to?
Goji extract is standardised to goji polysaccharides at 30%, 40% or 50%, measured by UV-VIS, from a water extraction of sun-dried Lycium barbarum fruit, spray-dried into a freely soluble powder with no added carriers in the pure grade. The powder is orange-red to red-brown, 80 mesh, with loss on drying limited to 5.0% or less.

What should I check when comparing goji extract specifications?
Check the assay grade first: 30%, 40% or 50% polysaccharides, stated with the UV-VIS method, because sheets written at different grades cannot be compared line by line. Then the carrier status (the pure grade has no added carriers), the mesh line (80 mesh), the loss-on-drying limit (5.0% or less), and the documentation: ask each supplier for the lot COA that would ship with your order rather than a generic sample. Zeaxanthin content is available on request if your formula needs it stated.

What packing and shelf life does goji extract come in?
Standard packing is a 25 kg fibre drum with a double polyethylene liner, with 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags for samples and pilot runs. Shelf life is 24 months in unopened original packing, stored cool, dry and protected from light. MOQ is 1 kg, and every lot ships with a batch COA.
