Where astragalus extract fits: four formula categories

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 3 November 2026 · 6 min read

The three questions we ask before recommending an astragalus application. Most enquiries arrive with a finished format in mind, and the grade follows from the format. So we ask which category the product sits in, whether the label claim rests on polysaccharides or astragaloside IV, and whether the process needs 80 mesh or 100 mesh. We then suggest the grade that matches the label with margin, tested on material drawn from a production lot. If the format needs a maltodextrin carrier or finer mesh, we confirm feasibility with our manufacturing partner before quoting.

Four application areas cover almost every astragalus enquiry we handle: capsules and tablets, adaptogen and daily-wellness blends, functional powders and stick packs, and tea or beverage concentrates. What changes is not the extract but three decisions — grade, mesh and carrier. The sheet itself is broken down in our astragalus specification guide, and the buying mechanics in our astragalus sourcing guide.

Immune-support capsules and tablets

Dry dose formats are the largest category we ship into, and the format question is whether the powder blends and stays blended through tableting. At 80 mesh, the standard grade, astragalus extract blends into direct-compression and granulation formulas without a separate milling stage; 100 mesh is available on request where the formula also carries finer actives. The light brown to yellow-brown colour works in an opaque or coated tablet, and the slightly sweet taste is mild enough that most capsules need no masking.

Grade choice follows the label. A product printing “astragalus polysaccharides 50%” should be built on the 50% polysaccharide grade with astragaloside IV at 0.5%, because that is the pairing the COA supports lot after lot. A product listing astragalus inside a multi-ingredient blend, without a polysaccharide claim, usually runs on 40% with 0.3% and keeps the extract as one line among several. We supply the extract as a food ingredient and make no medical claims; structure/function wording and label compliance are the buyer's responsibility.

Adaptogen and daily-wellness blends

Blends are where grade discipline matters most, because astragalus is rarely alone. A daily-wellness sachet might carry astragalus alongside poria, rhodiola or a vitamin premix, and the extract has to hold its place without dominating colour or taste. The 40% / 0.3% grade is the usual starting point: enough polysaccharide to justify inclusion on the panel, with a colour and flavour load that disappears into a blend.

Two practical notes. Declare the carrier: our standard material is a pure extract with no excipient, but maltodextrin can be added on request, and if the label carries a carbohydrate declaration the carrier changes those numbers. Fix the mesh before the blend is finalised, because the finest component sets the mesh the whole blend must pass; where the blend includes a 100 mesh active, running astragalus at 100 mesh avoids segregation in the hopper.

Functional powders and stick packs

Stick packs put a premium on dispersion rather than compression. The extract is water-extracted and disperses readily, so it reconstitutes without a separate solubilising step; at 80 mesh it disperses cleanly in a shaker bottle, and 100 mesh gives a smoother mouthfeel where the pack is drunk rather than stirred. Loss on drying at 5.0% or less, determined gravimetrically, is the line that protects the pack through its shelf life, since a single-serve sachet has a large surface area relative to its fill weight.

Flow is the other consideration. A powder with tight moisture control runs predictably on vertical form-fill-seal equipment; a lot at the top of the moisture limit can bridge in the hopper on a humid filling day. If your line runs in a high-humidity room, say so at quotation, and we can select a lot nearer the middle of the moisture range.

Herbal tea and beverage concentrates

Tea and concentrate formats are the most forgiving on mesh and the least forgiving on taste. The extract has a characteristic, slightly sweet taste that reads as pleasant in a botanical blend and as foreign in a fruit-forward drink, so most buyers position it in a herbal or traditional-style blend rather than a citrus or berry base. The 20% polysaccharide grade with 0.3% astragaloside IV is the usual choice: it carries the botanical identity at a lower polysaccharide load, which keeps the inclusion rate workable without a heavy powder note.

Water extraction matters here. Our standard grade is water-extracted, which keeps residual-solvent questions off the table for beverage labelling in most markets. A different extraction profile can be discussed with the manufacturing partner, but it is a different material and should be quoted as such.

The pattern is simple: higher polysaccharide grades for dry dose formats carrying a label claim, lower grades for blends and beverages where astragalus shares the panel. Start the trial with a 1 kg or 5 kg foil bag drawn from a production lot, and confirm carrier and mesh in writing before the formula is locked.

Frequently asked questions

What applications is astragalus extract used for?
Four categories: capsules and tablets, adaptogen and daily-wellness blends, functional powders and stick packs, and herbal tea or beverage concentrates. The product page lists the same set, and the water extraction and mild taste suit liquid formats as well as dry blends.

Which grade should I choose for my astragalus application?
Match the grade to the label. A product claiming polysaccharides 50% should use the 50% / 0.5% pairing; a multi-ingredient blend without a polysaccharide claim usually runs on 40% / 0.3%; tea and beverage concentrates typically use 20% / 0.3%. Polysaccharides are measured by UV-VIS, astragaloside IV by HPLC.

Which mesh and carrier should I specify?
80 mesh is standard and suits all four categories; 100 mesh is available on request for finer dispersion. The standard material is a pure extract with no excipient, and maltodextrin can be added on request. Declare the carrier if your label carries a carbohydrate figure.
