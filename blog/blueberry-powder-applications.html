Where blueberry powder earns its cost in a formula

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 18 October 2026 · 6 min read

What buyers send us before a colour trial. Nine out of ten blueberry powder enquiries arrive with a photo of a finished product and the question &ldquo;can you match this&rdquo;. A photo is a start, not a specification &mdash; so we send back the lot photo under the same light, the carrier status, and a 1 kg or 5 kg foil bag drawn from production, then let the buyer&rsquo;s own cook curve decide. The projects that fail are the ones where the colour was approved on a dry powder sample.

Blueberry powder is one of the few ingredients in the natural range that is bought for what it looks like as much as for what it contains. Spray-dried from ripe, high-anthocyanin fruit, it arrives as a vivid magenta-purple fine powder, and that colour is the reason most formulas include it. This article looks at the four formats the product page lists, what the powder contributes in each, and where the format pushes back.

Smoothie and juice blends

The easiest home for the material. It is water-soluble, it disperses at 80 mesh, and it brings fruit colour without a synthetic dye on the label. Two practical notes: colour in a liquid is judged against the liquid, so a scoop that looks generous in a dry blend can read thin in 300 ml, and the taste is characteristic of the fruit, which is an advantage in a smoothie and a problem in a neutral wellness drink. Taste the finished dilution, not the powder.

Gummies and confectionery

The hardest format, and the one where buyers ask the most questions. Gummy cooking is a heat and pH exercise, and anthocyanins are sensitive to both. A powder that looks right in the dry state can come out of the depositor a duller, browner shade after a long cook at low pH. That does not mean the ingredient is wrong — it means the process window needs to be established with your own trials. Ask for a sample from the production lot, run it in your actual recipe at your actual cook curve, and judge the colour after 48 hours, not straight off the line.

Bakery and cereal coatings

Here the powder is usually working on the surface rather than in the crumb, which changes the maths. Surface colour needs the powder to stay where it is put: a carrier-free grade gives a stronger colour per kilo, while a maltodextrin-carried grade can help adhesion and reduce dusting in a coating drum. Moisture matters too — at or below 5.0% loss on drying, the powder will not push a dry coating towards caking in the bag.

Color-forward nutraceutical blends

This is the fastest-growing use in our enquiries: a capsule or powder blend where the ingredient is the colour story on the front of the pack and one line in the supplement facts. Two things decide whether a blend works. First, the colour has to survive the blend — a vivid magenta powder disappears quickly next to green botanicals and dark mineral blends. Second, the label has to match the material, which is why carrier status should be agreed before the artwork, not after.

What to test in a pilot

Four tests will tell you more than a specification sheet can:

  • Colour in your matrix, measured after your process and again after 48 hours.
  • Dispersion at your real dilution, stirred the way your customer will stir it.
  • Flavour contribution at the level you intend to use, in the finished base.
  • Caking after a week in an open bag in your plant, not in a lab jar.

Packing for a pilot

Pilot work does not need a drum. The standard pack is a 25 kg fibre drum with a double polyethylene liner, and 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags are available for samples and pilot runs — drawn from production lots, so the material you trial is the material we would ship. In-stock items are ready in about two days; bulk production of standard grades runs 3–5 working days after deposit. If you are still deciding which grade of fruit powder to trial, the buyer’s guide covers the three products that share this name.

The question that decides most blueberry powder projects is not “how much per kilo” but “will this colour survive my process”. Answer that with a sample and a trial, not with a price list.

Frequently asked questions

What dosage forms is blueberry powder used in?
The product page lists smoothie and juice blends, gummies and confectionery, bakery and cereal coatings, and color-forward nutraceutical blends. It is a water-soluble, spray-dried fruit powder, so it suits liquid and confectionery formats as well as dry blends.

Will the colour survive gummy processing?
That depends on your cook temperature and pH, because anthocyanin colour is sensitive to both. Run a sample from a production lot through your own recipe and cook curve, then judge the shade after 48 hours. We supply 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags for exactly this kind of pilot work.

What packing and lead time apply to a pilot order?
The standard pack is a 25 kg fibre drum with a double polyethylene liner, with 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags for samples and pilot runs. In-stock items are ready in about 2 days; bulk production of standard grades takes 3-5 working days after deposit.
