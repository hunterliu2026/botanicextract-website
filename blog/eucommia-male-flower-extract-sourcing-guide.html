How to source eucommia male flower extract: the two-assay ingredient

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 2 November 2026 · 6 min read

One flower, two numbers, no standard grade. Eucommia male flower extract is unusual among the botanicals we ship in that its identity rests on two assay lines — total flavones and chlorogenic acid — and in that there is no single "standard" percentage the whole market quotes. That makes the first conversation with a supplier more important than the quotation, because the buyer has to arrive knowing what their own label needs before either number means anything.

Eucommia male flower extract comes from the flower of Eucommia ulmoides — the male flower of the hardy rubber tree — extracted with water or ethanol-water and released against a per-lot COA rather than a single market-standard grade. The category positions it across metabolism support, liver-kidney tonic products, calm-energy blends and men's tonic lines, which is a wide brief for one ingredient. This guide covers what decides quality here, what the specification should say, and the questions to settle before a first order. Related reading in the same category: our epimedium extract sourcing guide.

Why two assays instead of one

Most botanical extracts hang their identity on a single marker compound. Eucommia male flower carries two: total flavones and chlorogenic acid, both measured by HPLC and both reported per lot. The reason is that the flower's profile is genuinely two-dimensional — the flavone fraction and the chlorogenic-acid fraction contribute to different positioning, and a material strong on one axis and weak on the other is not interchangeable with one that balances both. For the buyer this means the first question is not "what percentage?" but "which of the two numbers does my label and my positioning need, and what minimum do I require on the other?" A supplier who can quote both lines per lot, consistently, is demonstrating exactly the discipline this ingredient requires.

What the specification should say

Our sheet reads as follows, and it is a fair checklist for comparing suppliers. Botanical source: Eucommia ulmoides male flower, part used flower. Extraction solvent: water or ethanol-water. Assays: total flavones and chlorogenic acid, each per lot COA by HPLC. Appearance: brown fine powder with characteristic odour and taste. Carrier: none — a pure extract — with maltodextrin available on request where the buyer's process needs a flowing carrier. Particle size: 80 mesh standard, 100 mesh on request. Loss on drying and total ash: each limited to 5.0%. Packing: 25 kg fibre drum with double polyethylene liner; 1 kg and 5 kg foil bags for samples and pilots. Shelf life: 24 months unopened, stored cool and dry.

The carrier question: pure extract or maltodextrin

Most buyers take the pure extract — no carrier — and manage flow in their own blending. But the maltodextrin option exists for a real reason: Eucommia male flower extract is hygroscopic enough that in humid production environments or in stick-pack formats, a small carrier fraction saves more in process hassle than it costs in label space. The decision belongs to the buyer's process, and the specification sheet should offer both rather than lock one in. What should not be ambiguous is which one the quotation covers — a price quoted on a carried material is not comparable to one quoted on a pure extract, because part of what you are buying is maltodextrin.

The questions to settle before your first order

Four questions do the work. First, your own label: which assay line leads your positioning, and what minimum do you need on both? Second, the method consistency: both assays by HPLC, stated identically on datasheet and lot COA — this ingredient has no single standard grade, so per-lot consistency is the whole game. Third, the physicals: mesh, loss on drying, total ash, and whether the quotation is for pure extract or a carried version. Fourth, lead time: in-stock material is ready in about 2 days and bulk production takes 3 to 5 working days after deposit; transit from Xi'an runs about 6 days by air or 18 to 25 days by sea after consolidation in Shenzhen. As always in this category, samples are drawn from production lots so that what you trial is what you will receive.

Frequently asked questions

What is eucommia male flower extract standardised to?
Two lines, both by HPLC and both per lot COA: total flavones and chlorogenic acid. There is no single market-standard percentage — the buyer sets the minimum on each line according to their label and positioning. The extract comes from the flower of Eucommia ulmoides, extracted with water or ethanol-water.

Should I take the pure extract or the maltodextrin-carried version?
Take the pure extract for clean labels and standard blending; take the maltodextrin version when your process needs better flow — humid environments or stick-pack formats especially. The quotation must state which one it covers, because a carried material is not price-comparable to a pure extract.

What is the MOQ, sample policy and lead time?
MOQ is 1 kg. Samples are drawn from production lots; how much we can supply free depends on the item's value and the rest is billed at cost plus courier, quoted in advance. In-stock items ship in about 2 days, bulk production 3 to 5 working days after deposit, and transit is about 6 days by air or 18 to 25 days by sea from Xi'an via Shenzhen consolidation.
