How to source hawthorn extract without guessing the grade

By Hunter Liu · BotanicExtract (Xi'an Yuanqingyuan Technology Co., Ltd.) · Published 9 November 2026 · 6 min read

Hawthorn extract is one of the most recognised botanicals on the shelf, and one of the easiest to mis-buy, because the market sells several Crataegus species under the same name at different flavonoid levels. The grade we supply is Hawthorn Powder — a concentrated extract of Chinese hawthorn fruit (Crataegus pinnatifida Bge.) standardised on total flavonoids at 5% or 10% by UV-VIS, extracted with food-grade water. This guide covers how to source hawthorn extract: what the material is, the specification line by line, what it is bought for, and what a first order looks like. If you are comparing several suppliers at once, the mechanics below are the same ones we apply to every hawthorn extract quotation that leaves this desk.

What hawthorn extract is

Three lines on the sheet matter before any price is compared. First, the species: Crataegus pinnatifida is the Chinese hawthorn, the higher-flavonoid species, and it is what separates this grade from material of unstated species sold under the same name. Second, the part and process: the fruit, extracted with food-grade water, which is what buyers with clean-label positions ask about first. Third, the assay: total flavonoids 5% or 10%, measured by UV-VIS spectrophotometry. The result is a red-brown fine powder with a fruity-acid taste — the taste is part of the material, and buyers of digestive bitters actually want it forward rather than masked.

Two lines on this sheet do more work than they look like they do. The marker line — vitexin-2″-O-rhamnoside, available on request — is what buyers with a marker-based label claim add to the sheet, and it is worth asking for up front rather than after the first order. And the loss-on-drying line, limited to 5.0% or less, is the physical limit the lot COA reports against alongside the flavonoid assay. Every lot ships with a batch COA; on an indicative standard lot, total flavonoids (UV) come in around 5.6% on the 5% grade, loss on drying around 4.5%, lead below 1 ppm, total plate count below 1,000 cfu/g, and E. coli and Salmonella negative — the actual COA for your batch is issued with the shipment.

What hawthorn extract is bought for

Three application areas cover almost every enquiry we see, and the product page lists them plainly:

  • Cardiovascular-support formulas — the home positioning; hawthorn carries long consumer recognition in both European phytomedicine and Chinese tradition, and buyers use that recognition in their own label positioning
  • Cholesterol-maintenance compositions — formulated as part of multi-ingredient blends where the flavonoid assay is the specification anchor
  • Digestive bitters and enzyme blends — where the fruity-acid taste of the fruit extract is a feature of the format rather than something to mask

We supply hawthorn as a raw ingredient for manufacturing, and how the finished product is positioned and labelled is the buyer's responsibility; our commitment is the specification, the batch COA and the documentation behind them. Buyers in the same documentation standard also source our green tea extract and mulberry leaf extract lines, and the three often sit in adjacent SKUs of the same range.

If your specification sets a flavonoid level or a marker assay outside the standard 5% / 10% grades, tell us the target specification and we confirm feasibility and lead time with our manufacturing partner within one business day. When you shortlist a hawthorn extract supplier, the species line and the lot COA are the two documents that separate sheets from results.

Frequently asked questions

What is hawthorn extract standardised to?
The standard grades are total flavonoids 5% and 10%, measured by UV-VIS spectrophotometry, from Crataegus pinnatifida fruit extracted with food-grade water. The powder is red-brown and fine with a fruity-acid taste, at 80 mesh, with loss on drying limited to 5.0% or less. A vitexin-2″-O-rhamnoside assay can be produced on request.

What should I check when comparing hawthorn extract specifications?
Check the botanical line first: Crataegus pinnatifida is the higher-flavonoid Chinese hawthorn species, and sheets written against other Crataegus species cannot be compared with it. Then the assay basis (total flavonoids 5% / 10% by UV-VIS), whether a marker assay such as vitexin-2″-O-rhamnoside is available, the mesh line (80 mesh), and the physical limits (loss on drying 5.0% or less). Ask each supplier for the lot COA that would ship with your order rather than a generic sample.

What is the MOQ, sample policy and lead time for hawthorn extract?
MOQ is 1 kg. Samples are always drawn from production lots; the free quantity is agreed by item value, and anything beyond is billed at cost plus courier and quoted up front. In-stock items are ready in about 2 days, bulk production takes 3 to 5 working days after deposit, and transit is about 3 days Xi'an to Shenzhen plus about 6 days by air to major U.S. and EU airports, or 18 to 25 days by sea.
